Can the detector tell which side is Human?

YOLO11n trained on synthetic renders only, evaluated on 127 real venue photos (Aug 12 shoot) · model v25f2 · 2026-08-18

Verdict: yes — at close range. At the distance where the robot actually makes its sorting decision (bounding box ≥ ~25% of image height), left/right Human-vs-Robot classification was 100% correct in this photo set. Errors exist, but they are all far-range, all in one direction (Human misread as Robot), and two of the three failure causes are fixable in post-processing without retraining.

01What was tested

The competition places two visually near-identical wooden mannequins on the goals: the HUMAN dummy wears grey clothes with bare wood showing, the ROBOT dummy is painted silver. The robot only needs one bit of information per goal pair: which side is Human, which side is Robot. Exact boxes don't matter.

03Failure cause 1 — duplicate boxes, not real errors

Half of the "wrong" frames weren't wrong at all. YOLO's default duplicate suppression (NMS) runs per class, so one dummy can receive both a Human box and a Robot box at the same spot — which our left-to-right reading then counts as two dummies. 27 such phantom boxes appeared across the set.

Applying the dedupe lifted seated-burst wrong frames from 13 down to 7. This fix is free — no retraining.

Two things jump out:

The error is one-directional. The silver Robot dummy was never once called Human. Every mistake is the grey-clothed Human dummy being called Robot, and only when small in frame. This matches our material measurements: under the venue's lime-green bounce light, grey cloth and silver paint are nearly the same color — the only reliable cue is the warm bare-wood head and hands, and at 120 px tall the head is ~15 px, too small to carry the decision. (The synthetic Human also has no garment mesh yet, so silhouette can't help either.)

It vanishes at decision range. Our arrival logic fires when the box reaches ~28% of image height — comfortably inside the 260+ px bucket where accuracy was 100/100.

Useful asymmetry for decision logic When this model says HUMAN, it has never been wrong in this set (100% precision). When it says ROBOT at long range, there's a real chance it's actually the Human dummy. Any voting or symmetry logic should weight Human calls heavily and distrust far-range Robot calls.

05Failure cause 3 — misses, and the standing bursts

Five seated frames produced no detection at all, almost all in the farthest burst (172414). In one-detection frames the missing dummy was the right-side silver one 25 times vs 8 for the left — partly the photo composition (the silver dummy sat against background clutter), possibly also its low contrast against grey furniture. A couple of thin false boxes hugged the frame edge; a 3% border margin filter removes them.

The standing bursts (173126/173204) read almost entirely as Robot. This is not a model defect: standing placements are illegal under the rulebook, so the training pose space excludes them on purpose, and these photos exist only as appearance references. For the future 4-dummy symmetric arrangement, the plan is a layer above the detector: combine the known H-R-H-R symmetry with per-box confidence — and per §04, give Human calls the benefit of the doubt.

06What we do about it

  1. Inference settings: confidence threshold 0.25 (not 0.5 — halving the threshold nearly doubled recall with no visible false-positive cost), plus agnostic_nms=True or an equivalent cross-class IoU dedupe in post-processing, plus a 3% frame-edge filter.
  2. Decision policy: latch the left/right verdict only at close range (box ≥ ~25% of image height) and only after a majority vote over several frames. Never trust a single far-range frame — frame-to-frame flicker in this handheld set was ~94%.
  3. Root fix (training side): the far-range Human→Robot flip won't improve with more epochs — training already auto-stopped on a plateau at mAP50 0.987. It needs a garment mesh on the synthetic Human dummy and more small-scale figures in the render mix. Scheduled for dataset v2.
